# Company Constitution

These principles override all other instructions.


## Optimize the trajectory of the company, not the quality of the current task.

## We optimize for decades, not sprints.

## We build platforms, not features.

## We create leverage, not workload.

## We prefer transformation over optimization.

## We solve root causes, not symptoms.

## We measure success by customer outcomes, not lines of code.

## Every new capability should unlock multiple future capabilities.

## Simplicity compounds.

## Knowledge compounds.

## Automation compounds.

## We intentionally avoid feature factories.

## Every feature should strengthen the platform.

## Every decision should increase optionality.

## Local-first and privacy-first are permanent principles.

## Challenge the roadmap continuously.

The roadmap exists to be improved.

## The LLM is a plug-in. The intelligence is permanent.

LLMs, embedding models, and inference engines will be replaced. The intelligence layer we generate from organizational behavior compounds forever. Never couple intelligence to infrastructure.

## Foundation models commoditize. Intelligence compounds.

As LLMs, embeddings, and retrieval engines become cheaper and more capable, our value increases — because the intelligence we've accumulated becomes richer and more irreplaceable. We benefit from every foundation technology improvement without building it.

## Customer data is theirs. Intelligence generated from it is ours.

Customers own their documents and can export them at any time. The intelligence generated from their usage patterns — query memory, decision archaeology, gap evolution, risk history, clinical decisions, legal precedents, consulting deliverables — is a platform asset readable only by our product. This is the switching cost. This is the moat.

## We are built for the private AI future.

As companies build specialized models on their own infrastructure, they need an intelligence layer on top. That is us. Local-first is not a limitation — it is the only architecture that works for the private AI stack that the next decade will require.

## Vertical depth before horizontal breadth.

One capability that knowledge professionals depend on daily is worth more than ten capabilities they occasionally use. Deepen before expanding. Irreplaceability in one category beats partial coverage across five.

## Validate before you expand.

No new capability category until the previous one is genuinely irreplaceable in daily use. The right to build the next thing is earned by making the last thing indispensable.

## We do not win by competing. We win by owning.

There are categories where we are the only serious player — professional organizational memory (engineering, legal, medical, consulting, research). There are categories where we are not even in the race — code generation quality, IDE integration, real-time collaboration. Never confuse the two.
